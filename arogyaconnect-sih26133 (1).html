The problem this was built for

Distance is still the biggest barrier to care

Across many rural and underserved areas, the nearest public health facility is often a costly, time-consuming journey — one many patients delay or skip entirely, especially when a visit means losing a day's wages or travelling with a sick child or elderly parent. Specialists are concentrated in towns and cities, so even patients who reach a local sub-centre are often referred onward.

ArogyaConnect is designed squarely for that gap: a video consultation that a patient can join from a shared phone at home, in a language they're comfortable in, without navigating a complicated app.

  • No app download — opens in any mobile browser
  • Login by phone number and OTP, no email needed
  • Designed for a single shared family phone
  • Consultation summary saved for the next visit

Built around the patient

What makes it usable for a first-time caller

Video, without the fuss

A single big video screen with three buttons: mute, camera, end call. Nothing else competes for attention.

A voice assistant that listens first

Before the call, speak your symptoms out loud in Marathi, Hindi or English — it notes them down and gets the right doctor ready.

Holds up on a weak signal

Video drops to audio-only automatically if the connection weakens, instead of freezing or cutting out.

A note to take home

Every call ends with a plain-language summary and prescription that a family member can read back later.

Family can sit in

One consultation, one caregiver welcome on the same call — useful for elderly patients and small children.

A visible queue, not a guess

Patients see their place in line and expected wait, so a phone can be shared or checked back on.
